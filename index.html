Causal perturbations · cell state · control

Youssef Naciri Zerta

Can we predictably control what a cell does? I want to understand what must be learned from perturbations to design interventions that produce a specified cellular function.

My current work asks which experiments distinguish regulatory mechanisms that matter for intervention choice, whether representations of cellular state predict unseen perturbations, and how to evaluate such predictions against experimental evidence. I started in wet-lab biotechnology; my research now combines causal inference, dynamical modelling, single-cell data and scientific software. Longer term, I am interested in both redirecting endogenous regulation and programming new cellular responses.

Thesis · perturbation biology · cell-state control

Which perturbations identify mechanisms that matter for control?

EMBL-EBI, Saez Lab, with the Wellcome Sanger Institute · with Rory Maizels

I simulate published gene-regulatory networks with ordinary differential equations and realistic count noise, where the underlying mechanism is known. I test which perturbation protocols distinguish competing mechanisms and, more importantly, whether the remaining ambiguity changes a prediction or the intervention one would choose. The current comparison includes knockdowns, knockouts and calibrated, state-dependent increases in transcription-factor production, analogous to enhancer-driven constructs.

The next part moves to perturbation data from differentiating stem cells. I plan to learn causal representations of regulatory state, evaluate predictions for interventions held out from training, and investigate the selection of interventions toward a target cell state. This work is ongoing; prospective functional control remains the goal, not a result already established.

Research project 09/2026–02/2027, remote · thesis 02–08/2027, full time on site

Biological networks · infrastructure

AnnNet and SysBioVerse

EMBL-EBI, Saez Lab, with Open Targets · lead developer

AnnNet is AnnData for biological networks. One annotated container holds directed, signed, hyperedge, multilayer and condition-specific structure, with lossless storage and interoperability with NetworkX, igraph, graph-tool and PyTorch Geometric. A plain directed graph is only one special case, which matters because biological interactions often change with context, and a single graph throws that context away. I designed and built the core: the incidence representation, the annotation model, the storage format and the library interfaces. Groups at EMBL-EBI and Imperial College London use it, and it is developed with scverse core developers so that it fits the single-cell Python ecosystem.

It is now growing into SysBioVerse (2026–), a network-biology consortium with scverse: one interface for regulatory, signalling, metabolic, cell–cell and multi-omic causal inference, so that methods which model biological structure differently can be compared and combined instead of being reimplemented for each paper.

Lead developer · co-first author, preprint forthcoming

Experimental systems biology · interaction networks

Multiplex bacterial interaction networks

Université Euromed de Fès

A trait-resolved interaction study across 60 soil bacterial strains, which I designed and ran: pairwise assays producing 12 directed interaction layers, analysed as one multilayer network rather than a single graph. The analysis places the strains in a low-dimensional space of antagonistic, metabolic and morphogenetic strategies, and shows that the sign and strength of an interaction depend on context rather than being fixed for a pair of strains. That result is where AnnNet started: a single directed graph cannot represent a system whose edges change meaning with conditions.

First author · bioRxiv preprint · under review, ISME Communications

Interpretability · null calibration

Sparsity, not alignment

Sole author

Do the “features” that sparse autoencoders find in a genomic foundation model correspond to regulatory mechanism? I tested this against per-base causal ground truth from saturation-mutagenesis massively parallel reporter assays. Run the standard way, the test reports a discovery that survives every control such claims usually get: label shuffling, repeat masking, a likelihood baseline, three seeds, two autoencoder families and two architectures. The discovery is an artifact. The test thresholds one tail of a null distribution, and the same false-discovery-rate procedure applied to the other tail finds even more “discoveries”. The two distributions differ in spread, not in location, and the effect grows with dictionary size, because sparser units simply have noisier scores. Replicating across seeds and architectures rules out a coding error, not a wrong null, which is why the usual controls could not catch it.

Sole author · accepted, NeurIPS 2026 workshop · paper

Cellular state · representation learning

SampleCLR: patient-level representations from single-cell data

Helmholtz Munich, Lücken Lab · research collaborator

A model combining contrastive learning and multi-head attention that learns patient-level embeddings from single-cell transcriptomics; the paper reports state-of-the-art results on disease-stratification benchmarks. I contributed the interpretability and transfer-learning analyses and parts of the architecture design, including tests of whether the representations transfer across biological systems rather than only fitting the benchmark.

Research questions

These are the problems I want to investigate, not claims that I have solved them.

01 /

Which uncertainty matters for intervention?

Several mechanisms may explain the same observations. But we do not always need to identify every interaction: we need to resolve the differences that would change an intervention decision. Which experiments are most informative for that decision, rather than merely for reconstructing a network?

Experimental design · causal identifiability · perturbation response
02 /

What description of cell state survives an unseen perturbation?

Genes are measurable, but intervention effects may be coordinated across genes and change over time. Regulatory activities, programmes and dynamical responses might be more useful for prediction. Learned latent features are not exempt from scrutiny: my work on model interpretation showed how convincing features can arise from evaluation artifacts. I want representations that earn their meaning by predicting experimental responses.

Causal representation learning · dynamics · out-of-distribution evaluation
03 /

Can we design a change in cellular function, not just in expression?

A desired transcriptional signature is not sufficient evidence of a functional or stable cell state. The longer-term test is to select interventions in advance, measure what the cells actually do, and understand failures across contexts. I am interested in both manipulating endogenous regulation and designing synthetic regulatory systems when existing control mechanisms are insufficient.
