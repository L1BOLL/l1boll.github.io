Causal systems biology · infrastructure

SysBioVerse

Open Targets — EMBL-EBI, Saez Lab · lead developer

A unified ecosystem for methods that infer causal and mechanistic biological structure from omics data and prior knowledge: the intended equivalent of scverse for causal network inference. The problem is representational before it is engineering. Methods disagree about what a causal biological structure even is, and a shared interface has to expose enough that regulatory, signalling, metabolic, cell–cell and multi-omic outputs can be compared, benchmarked and composed within one system, rather than reimplemented per paper against incompatible data structures.

Lead developer · Open Targets
02

Biological networks · structured representations

AnnNet

EMBL-EBI, Saez Lab · lead developer

An AnnData-style annotated container for complex biological networks: directed, signed, hyperedge, multilayer and condition-specific topology on a single sparse incidence core, with typed annotation tables, lossless native storage, and lazy interoperability with NetworkX, igraph, graph-tool and PyTorch Geometric. The classical directed graph is a special case rather than the frame — which matters, because forcing biological structure into it discards exactly the conditioning that makes a network claim causal. Used by groups at EMBL-EBI and Imperial College London, and anchors SysBioVerse.

Co-first author · in preparation · target: Bioinformatics

SampleCLR — patient-level representations from single-cell data

Helmholtz Munich, Lücken Lab · research collaborator

An architecture combining contrastive learning and multi-head attention to learn patient-level embeddings from single-cell transcriptomics, reaching state-of-the-art results on disease-stratification benchmarks. I contributed interpretability and transfer-learning analyses, and to the architecture design. 04

Representational convergence · interventional grounding

Testing representational convergence against interventional ground truth

Sole author

A widely reported measure of cross-modal representational convergence is read against a baseline that is itself capacity-dependent: the baseline rises with the very variable the claim is about. The corrected null follows in closed form, and once it is subtracted the reported scaling trend does not survive. The question is decided on a biological testbed rather than a vision–language one — a coding variant is two exact views of a single object, related by the genetic code and labelled by saturation genome editing, so every term of the null is measured rather than assumed. Using a causal biological assay to settle a question the machine-learning literature could not is the method I want to generalise.

Sole author · in preparation, ICLR 2027 · title withheld during double-blind review
05

Causal evaluation · identifiability

A causal signal without causal features

Sole author

Whether the interpretability “features” read out of a genomic foundation model can be trusted, tested against per-base-pair causal ground truth from saturation-mutagenesis MPRA. A real aggregate causal signal survives the full control suite — but the per-feature decomposition does not. Causal features barely reproduce across retrains, a second autoencoder family recovers a near-disjoint set from identical activations, and a single supervised direction stably decodes what the decomposition could not. The failure is the decomposition, not the model, and it is a constraint on any latent account of gene regulation rather than a verdict on one method.

Sole author · under review, MLCB 2026 · preprint “A Causal Signal Without Causal Features”

Experimental systems biology · interaction structure

Multiplex bacterial interaction networks

Université Euromed de Fès

A trait-resolved interaction study across 60 soil bacterial strains, generating 12 directed interaction layers from large-scale pairwise assays — an interaction tensor rather than a single network. A multilayer analysis of that tensor yields a low-dimensional ecological embedding classifying microbial strategies across antagonistic, metabolic and morphogenetic modes, and shows that interaction sign and strength are context-dependent rather than intrinsic to a strain pair. This is where the limitation motivating AnnNet came from: a single directed graph cannot represent a system whose edges change meaning with condition.

First author · in preparation · target: ISME J

Regulation in the wrong coordinates

The question I keep returning to is not what regulates what, but in which coordinates that question is well-posed. The gene is a measurement channel biology handed us, not necessarily a causal joint of the system: several distinct maps get conflated under the word, and only one of them — from an intervention at a locus to a displacement of cell state — is genuinely many-to-many. Genes remain perfectly real objects. They may simply be the wrong basis for the causal claim.

Two long-standing puzzles collapse into one if that is right. Pleiotropy is one gene touching many things; polygenicity is one thing touched by many genes. These need not be two mechanisms requiring two explanations — they can be the same distributed structure read along its rows or along its columns, and missing heritability then looks less like absent information than like variance that appears scattered because the causal units are not gene-shaped. The general reflex: when a field has a stubborn residual, suspect the coordinate system before going looking for more variance.

What the data has forced so far goes a step further than I expected, and cuts against my own starting position. A learned feature basis is also just a chart. There is no inventory of latent features waiting to be found — resolution is a knob rather than a discovery — which is why a plain gene–gene correlation matrix can outperform a learned overcomplete dictionary: both are coordinate systems on the same continuum, and the gene basis at least carries no estimation error. Three times now, on different statistics, a quantity that looked directly measurable turned out to be manufactured by the act of measuring it. That is not three bugs; it is a constraint on latent-variable accounts of regulation in general.

So the direction is not gene basis to feature basis. It is toward the objects that are measured rather than inferred: how a system fluctuates on its own, and how it moves when it is pushed. Regulation is the operator between them, and the interesting biology is where the relation between fluctuation and response breaks down. What I want to know is whether that breakage is structured, and what invariant is left to hunt when the rank of the problem turns out not to exist.
