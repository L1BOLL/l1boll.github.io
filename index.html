Cell state · intervention · control

Youssef Naciri Zerta

Given a cell in one state and a target state, I want an intervention that moves it there to be derived, not found by screening. Everything below is what stands in the way of that.

That is a question about measurement before it is a question about method: what a cell’s state actually is, which interventions carry information about it, and at what level of organisation causal relationships become predictable rather than descriptive. I work on it with causal representation learning and identifiability theory, mechanistic and dynamical modelling, perturbational genomics, and the infrastructure that makes causal inference over biological structure comparable — tools for the question rather than the subject of it.

Perturbation biology · identifiability

Can the interventions we perform identify the mechanism we claim?

EMBL-EBI, Saez Lab · with Rory Maizels (Saez Lab, EMBL-EBI; Parts Group, Sanger; Briscoe Group, The Crick)

Perturbation biology assumes that transcriptomic responses to gene-level interventions are an adequate basis for learning regulatory mechanism. I test that from the identifiability side: which aspects of a regulatory system can in principle be recovered from the interventions actually performed, where information is missing rather than merely noisy, and whether apparent model performance survives systems with known causal ground truth. The instrument is a simulator — ODE models of published gene regulatory networks spanning monostable, multistable and oscillatory dynamics, with a count-sampling observation model — under five intervention classes, of which only knockdown and knockout are node interventions expressible as do(·) on observed variables; the rest act on the mechanism. Bayesian inference is run per class and scored on structure recovery, parameter recovery and held-out prediction, and the failure signatures are then looked for in public Perturb-seq data.

The second phase moves from node-level to calibrated mechanism-level intervention measured in cells, where what is delivered is a function of cell state rather than the identity of a gene — the first step toward deriving an intervention rather than selecting one from a screen.

Research project 09/2026–02/2027, remote · thesis 02–08/2027, full time at EMBL-EBI

Interpretability · null calibration

Sparsity, not alignment

Sole author

Whether the interpretability “features” read out of a genomic foundation model can be trusted, tested against per-base-pair causal ground truth from saturation-mutagenesis MPRA. Run as the field would run it, the test returns a discovery, and it passes every control such a claim is normally asked for: label shuffling, repeat masking, a likelihood baseline, three seeds, two autoencoder families, two architectures. The discovery is an artifact. The procedure thresholds one tail of a null and never asks whether the excess is one-sided — it is not, the same multiple-testing procedure on the opposite tail returns more discoveries, the two distributions differ in width rather than location, and the effect grows with dictionary size. What the threshold selects is units whose score is noisier than a dense random direction’s, which sparser units mechanically are. Replication across seeds and architectures is evidence against implementation error, not against a misspecified null — which is why the whole control suite could not see it.

Sole author · under review, NeurIPS 2026 workshop · earlier preprint

Representational convergence · baselines

The null scales too

Sole author

The surviving evidence for cross-modal representational convergence is local neighbourhood overlap read against a baseline that assumes evaluation points are exchangeable. They are not: points come in groups, grouped points are disproportionately mutual neighbours in both views at once, and capacity buys within-view clustering — so the baseline rises with the very variable the claim is about. The corrected null follows in closed form and reduces to the standard one as clustering vanishes, and permutation calibration provably cannot recover the missing term. The question is decided on a biological testbed rather than a vision–language one: a coding variant is two exact views of a single object, related by the genetic code, grouped by residue position and labelled by saturation genome editing, so every term of the null is measured rather than assumed. The null then moves at least as far as the raw metric along every capacity ladder, and what survives calibration does not replicate in sign.

Sole author · in preparation, ICLR 2027

Biological networks · structured representations

AnnNet

EMBL-EBI, Saez Lab, with Open Targets · lead developer

AnnData for systems biology. One annotated container holds directed, signed, hyperedge, multilayer and condition-specific structure on a single sparse incidence core, with typed annotation tables, lossless native storage and lazy interoperability with NetworkX, igraph, graph-tool and PyTorch Geometric. The classical directed graph is a special case rather than the frame — which matters, because forcing biological structure into it discards exactly the conditioning that makes a network claim causal. I designed and built the core: the incidence representation, the annotation model, the storage format and the library interfaces. It is used by groups at EMBL-EBI and Imperial College London, and developed in close contact with scverse core developers and collaborators, so that it composes with the single-cell stack rather than competing with it.

SysBioVerse (2026–, Open Targets) is the layer on top: the methods, and the connectors to the prior-knowledge ecosystem, that make AnnNet biology-aware. Regulatory, signalling, metabolic, cell–cell and multi-omic causal inference behind one interface, so that methods which disagree about what a causal biological structure even is can still be compared, benchmarked and composed — rather than reimplemented per paper against incompatible data structures.

Lead developer · co-first author, in preparation · target: Bioinformatics

Cellular state · representation learning

SampleCLR — patient-level representations from single-cell data

Helmholtz Munich, Lücken Lab · research collaborator

An architecture combining contrastive learning and multi-head attention to learn patient-level embeddings from single-cell transcriptomics, reaching state-of-the-art results on disease-stratification benchmarks. I contributed interpretability and transfer-learning analyses, and to the architecture design. Whether a representation transfers across biological systems rather than fitting its benchmark is the question my later work formalises.

Experimental systems biology · interaction structure

Multiplex bacterial interaction networks

Université Euromed de Fès

A trait-resolved interaction study across 60 soil bacterial strains, generating 12 directed interaction layers from large-scale pairwise assays — an interaction tensor rather than a single network. A multilayer analysis of that tensor yields a low-dimensional ecological embedding classifying microbial strategies across antagonistic, metabolic and morphogenetic modes, and shows that interaction sign and strength are context-dependent rather than intrinsic to a strain pair. This is where the limitation motivating AnnNet came from: a single directed graph cannot represent a system whose edges change meaning with condition.

First author · in preparation · target: ISME J

Regulation in the wrong coordinates

The question I keep returning to is not what regulates what, but in which coordinates that question is well-posed. The gene is a measurement channel biology handed us, not necessarily a causal joint of the system: several distinct maps get conflated under the word, and only one of them — from an intervention at a locus to a displacement of cell state — is genuinely many-to-many. Genes remain perfectly real objects. They may simply be the wrong basis for the causal claim.

Two long-standing puzzles collapse into one if that is right. Pleiotropy is one gene touching many things; polygenicity is one thing touched by many genes. These need not be two mechanisms requiring two explanations — they can be the same distributed structure read along its rows or along its columns, and missing heritability then looks less like absent information than like variance that appears scattered because the causal units are not gene-shaped. The general reflex: when a field has a stubborn residual, suspect the coordinate system before going looking for more variance.

What the data has forced so far goes a step further than I expected, and cuts against my own starting position. A learned feature basis is also just a chart. There is no inventory of latent features waiting to be found — resolution is a knob rather than a discovery, and the reported number of them tracks the dictionary you chose rather than anything about the system. Three times now, on different statistics, a quantity that looked directly measurable turned out to be manufactured by the act of measuring it: once by a threshold, once by a baseline, once by a preprocessing seed. That is not three bugs; it is a constraint on latent-variable accounts of regulation in general.

So the direction is not gene basis to feature basis. It is toward the objects that are measured rather than inferred: how a system fluctuates on its own, and how it moves when it is pushed. Regulation is the operator between them, and the interesting biology is where the relation between fluctuation and response breaks down. What I want to know is whether that breakage is structured, and what invariant is left to hunt when the rank of the problem turns out not to exist.
